The six sessions 🌊 · from a list of poses to a class that moves

The running case is Rosa, a constructed newly qualified teacher, and her 60-minute Thursday flow at the Harbour Room, a constructed community studio. Rosa, the studio, the library and every bench number are invented for teaching, and every page says so.

Not medical advice. These pages are about the order and pace of a class. They do not teach anyone to diagnose, treat or clear a student for exercise. A student with chest pain, dizziness, a recent injury or pain that worsens with movement should stop and talk to a clinician. Session 6 lists the warning signs and the referral route.

What is honest here 🧾

Flow teaching runs on habits and conventions, and only some of it has been measured. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus
Every bench figure: class time, transition units, floor trips, side balance, counter-poses, peak, cool-downModelled in your browser from a constructed library and a 1 to 5 effort scale; nothing measures a real student
"A breath takes about five seconds in class"Unknown. Adults breathe 12 to 18 times a minute at rest; no study we found measured breath rate during a moving flow class. Session 2
"Inhale to lengthen, exhale to fold"A practitioner convention, not a research finding. Session 2
"Most injuries happen in transitions"No source found. Never claimed here. What is measured: yoga was as safe as usual care and exercise in trials, and emergency rates rise steeply over 65. Sessions 1 and 6
"Vinyasa is a workout"Moderate on average in one 60-minute study (3.6 METs); most yoga measured is light, and sun salutations are the exception. Session 5
