# Official course map - learn-yoga-flow-design-with-phoebe

Internal build document (never linked from an audience page). Every number a page quotes must be
in this file with its tier. Sources read 2026-11-25 (PubMed abstracts via NCBI E-utilities, the
Cleveland Clinic page in full). Motor-learning and NHS rows are copied with their tiers from the
sibling map in learn-yoga-course-design-with-phoebe (read there 2026-10-12).

Bucket `move`, d3, single-track 6 x 45 min, audience everyone (aspiring and new yoga teachers).
Running case: **Rosa** (constructed), a newly qualified teacher building her 60-minute Thursday
flow class at **the Harbour Room** (constructed community studio). Every page labels both
constructed the first time they appear. Not medical advice: said on the landing page, session 1,
the bench and session 6.

Scope decided with Phoebe 2026-11-25: this course owns the craft BETWEEN poses (transitions,
breath as the clock, pacing, repetition within a class, the intensity wave, adapting live).
It takes the class arc, the peak-pose method and sun salutations from Yoga Theory session 7 as
inputs and never re-teaches them.

## Verified facts by source (tier, where used)

### Breath rate (s2)
- Cleveland Clinic, Vital signs (A, full page): "The normal respiratory rate for an adult at rest
  is 12 to 18 breaths per minute." That is 3.3 to 5 seconds a breath. Clinical reference page, not
  a study.
- Mason H et al 2013 Evid Based Complement Alternat Med 2013:743504 (A): 17 yoga-naive young healthy
  adults; slow breathing at 6 breaths/min (10 s a breath), equal or unequal in/out, with or without
  ujjayi (Sanskrit, "victorious": breathing against a slightly narrowed throat). Equal
  inspiration/expiration at 6/min gave the largest baroreflex rise and blood-pressure fall; ujjayi
  "requires greater effort than normal slow breathing". Seated lab protocol, not a moving class.
- Breath rate DURING a moving flow class: no study found that measured it (searched 2026-11-25).
  Teach as an unknown; the bench's 4, 5 and 6 s are planning values (C).
- Pairing breath with movement (inhale on lengthening or opening, exhale on folding or twisting):
  practitioner convention, taught in Yoga Theory s7. No outcome study. Never call it a finding.

### Intensity of flow (s5)
- Larson-Meyer 2016 Med Sci Sports Exerc 48(8):1558-69 (A): systematic review, 17 studies
  (indirect calorimetry). Yoga practice 3.3 +/- 1.6 METs (range 1.83 to 7.4); 2.9 +/- 0.8 without
  one outlier (7.4 METs, sun salutations). Individual poses 2.2 +/- 0.7 (1.4 to 4.0); breathing
  exercises 1.3 +/- 0.3. "Yoga is typically classified as a light-intensity physical activity";
  a few sequences, including sun salutations, reach moderate to vigorous.
- Hagins 2007 BMC Complement Altern Med 7:40 (A): 20 intermediate-to-advanced practitioners, 56
  minutes of beginner-level video hatha yoga in a respiratory chamber: 2.5 METs, 93.2 beats/min,
  49.4% of predicted max heart rate, 3.2 kcal/min; similar to walking at 3.2 km/h. Sun-salutation
  bouts over 10 minutes "may contribute some portion" of sufficiently intense activity.
- Sherman SA 2017 J Phys Act Health 14(8):597-605 (A): 38 adults (20 M, 18 F), 60-minute vinyasa
  vs treadmill walking: yoga 3.6 +/- 0.6 METs, brisk self-paced walking 4.4, heart-rate-matched
  walking 4.7. "YOGA meets the criteria for moderate-intensity physical activity." Energy cost
  lower than heart-rate-matched walking: heart rate overstates vinyasa's energy cost.
- Cheruka 2023 Int J Yoga Therap 33:Article 2 (A): 40 experienced adults, 60-minute vinyasa
  (Baptiste "Journey into Power"): METs and %APMHR differed across sequences (p < 0.001);
  integration and restorative sequences lowest heart rate; balancing and back-bending similar METs.
  No per-sequence numbers in the abstract: quote the pattern, not values.
- Mody 2011 J Bodyw Mov Ther 15(3):343-7 (A): 6 young adults trained 2+ years, four rounds of sun
  salutation: 80% HRmax in round 2, 84% in round 3, 90% in round 4; average 80%; VO2 26 ml/kg/min.
  Tiny trained sample: say so every time.
- Tsopanidou 2020 J Sports Med Phys Fitness 60(8):1110-7 (A): 24 moderately trained adults, 90-min
  group vinyasa in four sections; high-intensity sun salutation section highest energy rate; mean
  7.1 +/- 2.6 kcal/min estimated from heart rate (an estimate, not calorimetry).

### Repetition within a class (s5)
- Shea and Morgan 1979 (A abstract, sibling map): random practice retained and transferred better
  than blocked. Brady 2004 (A, sibling map): overall effect 0.38; applied settings 0.19. Guadagnoli
  and Lee 2004 (A, sibling map): challenge point. These are motor-skill lab and applied findings,
  owned by Yoga Course Design s3; cite briefly and link. "Repeat-and-add" mini-flows within one
  class: practitioner convention, our inference only.

### Safety base rates (s1, s6)
- Cramer 2015 Am J Epidemiol 182(4):281-93 (A): 94 RCTs (8,430 participants) reported adverse
  events; no difference vs usual care or exercise; more non-serious events than psychological or
  educational controls (OR 7.30); "yoga appears as safe as usual care and exercise".
- Swain and McGwin 2016 Orthop J Sports Med 4(11) (A): 29,590 yoga-related US emergency department
  injuries 2001 to 2014; trunk 46.6%, sprain/strain 45.0%; 2014 rate per 100,000 participants: 65+
  57.9, 18-44 11.9, 45-64 17.7.
- Wiese 2019 J Bodyw Mov Ther 23(3):479-88 (A): 2,620 self-selected survey respondents; 45% no
  injury; of injuries 28% mild, 63% moderate, 9% severe (4% of all); more years practising and
  teaching yoga predicted reporting an injury. Convenience sample: say so.
- Where in a class injuries happen (transitions vs holds): X, no source found. Never claim it.

### Referral (s6)
- NHS Back pain, reviewed 5 March 2026 (A, sibling map): urgent signs as listed there (both legs
  numb or weak, loss of feeling around genitals or anus, bladder or bowel changes, chest pain, pain
  after a serious accident: 999 or A&E). Teachers stop, adapt, and refer; never assess.

## Per-session coverage

1. A flow is a path, not a list (green). The four states (base, level, facing, side); transition
   units counted by hand on Rosa's first standing line; floor trips (10 vs 2); safety base rates
   (Cramer, Swain, Wiese). Seam: YT s7 arc and peak pose as inputs. Build-along: map Rosa's
   opening into states and units.
2. Breath is the clock (green). 12 to 18 at rest (3.3 to 5 s), 6/min in a seated lab (Mason);
   nobody measured a moving class; the 4/5/6 s clock; the talk budget; rung 5's 51/60/69. Build-
   along: count your own breath, then time a 20-minute block on paper.
3. Transitions that work (yellow). Stick-figure guides: dog to lunge (step through, hand assist,
   knee down), standing to floor, front-facing to side-facing; units for each; Stop-if lines;
   linking moves; why a transition is taught like a pose. Build-along: rewrite three of Rosa's
   worst moves.
4. The Flow Bench (yellow). Rules, ladder 1 to 5, breath-clock break, own sequence.
5. Repetition and the wave (orange). METs (Larson-Meyer, Hagins, Sherman 2017), sequences differ
   (Cheruka), sun salutation rounds climb (Mody, n = 6), heart rate overstates energy (Sherman);
   repeat-and-add; peak position and cool-down from the bench. Build-along: draw the wave.
6. Adapting a flow live (orange). Running long or short (the cut order), the 9-minute swing, a
   room that is struggling, options for a transition, Stop-if, referral (NHS), injury base rates
   by age (Swain). Build-along: Rosa's cut card.
