The Flow Bench

Three sessions of pen-and-paper counting, now done for you. The bench takes a whole class, item by item, and checks seven things a flow teacher worries about: does it fit the hour, how much of it is spent moving between poses, how often the room goes down to the floor, are both sides even, does every strong backbend get a counter-pose, where does the peak fall, and how long is the cool-down. Tonight you walk Rosa's five drafts one rung at a time, break the breath clock on purpose, and then run your own class.

Not medical advice, and nothing leaves the page. The bench checks the structure of a sequence against a constructed library. Part 1 · covers the bench rules

How the bench reads a class

8 min live

The bench is simple on purpose, so you can check any number with a pencil. A class is an ordered list of items: a pose, or a composite such as one round of a sun salutation, each with a side, a number of breaths and a number of rounds. Everything else is counted from that list.

LiveSeven numbers and what each protects4 min▶

Class time protects the people who have a train to catch: holds plus moves plus a fixed 10-minute talk budget for the welcome, demonstrations and props, against a 60-minute target with one minute's grace. Transition units and floor trips protect the room's attention and its slowest movers (session 1). Side balance protects the body's sense that both sides were treated alike: a one-sided pose held 10 breaths on the right and 6 on the left is a common slip when the teacher is improvising.

Uncountered backbends follow a widespread practitioner habit: after a strong backbend (effort 3 or more on the bench: Locust, Bridge, Camel) offer a gentle fold or a rest within the next three items. It is a convention, not a tested rule, and the bench says so. Peak and cool-down describe the wave session 5 explores: where the hardest moment falls and how long the room gets to come down before rest.

Self-studyWhat the bench deliberately ignores3 min▶

It does not know your students. Effort levels are teaching judgements on a 1 to 5 scale: two experienced teachers could place Chair or Triangle a level apart, and a person with tight hamstrings will find Standing forward fold harder than the label says. It does not know how long a real move takes; one unit costing one breath is a planning assumption, and session 2 showed nobody has measured breath rate in a moving class. And it does not judge whether a sequence is beautiful, which is still your job.

The arc of one class and the peak-pose method come from Yoga Theory session 7 ↗; the bench only measures the arc you chose. To check a multi-week programme instead of a single class, use the programme bench in Yoga Course Design session 4 ↗.

Real world

The class plan written on a sticky note. Many teachers keep the night's flow on a small card at the front of the mat: pose names, maybe breath counts, nothing about the moves. That card is exactly the list the bench reads. Typing it in once, before the first time you teach it, is the fastest way to find the uneven side or the missing counter-pose before a room does.

Build-along · the bench

Rosa's five drafts, rung by rung

★ 22 min · everyone builds

Start on rung 1 at 5 seconds a breath. Read the verdict line, then the eight tiles, then the chart. Move one rung at a time and say out loud which number moved and which way. Then press the two breath-clock buttons on rung 5. Last, switch to "My own sequence", type your class and run it.

Rung 1 to rung 2: one number, one fix. Rung 1 holds Warrior II for 10 breaths on the first right side and 6 on the left, so across both passes the right gets 13 and the left 9. Rung 2 evens it to 6 and 6. Only the side-balance tile changes, and class time drops by 20 seconds.

★ Step 1 · read the changeRung 1: 64:05, Warrior II 13 R / 9 L, 3 uncountered, 10 floor trips Rung 2: 63:45, sides even, 3 uncountered, 10 floor trips

Rung 3: counter-poses cost time. Adding Child's pose after Locust and Knees to chest after each Bridge takes uncountered backbends from 3 to 0. It also pushes the class to 66:35. Good fixes rarely come free.

★ Step 2 · the price of a fixRung 3: 66:35 (+6:35), 0 uncountered, 74 units, 10 floor trips

Rung 4: reorder, do not cut. Grouping by level drops floor trips from 10 to 2 and transition units from 74 to 60. It does not fix the time, 66:25, because the poses are almost all still there. The peak moves from Camel at 58 percent to Warrior III at 53 percent, and the cool-down grows from 9:15 to 14:20.

★ Step 3 · same poses, better pathRung 4: 66:25, 60 units, largest move 3, 2 floor trips, peak Warrior III at 53%, cool-down 14:20

Rung 5: cut to the hour. Two fewer rounds of Sun salutation A and one fewer of B, one Locust instead of two, and shorter holds in arrival, the standing balances and the hip openers bring the class to exactly 60:00 at 5 seconds a breath, with every other check still clean.

★ Step 4 · on timeRung 5: 60:00 = holds 45:20 + moves 4:40 + talk 10:00 0 uneven, 0 uncountered, 2 floor trips, peak 52%, cool-down 13:30

Break it: change the breath clock. On rung 5, press 4 seconds, then 6 seconds. Nothing in the sequence changed. Watch the class time.

★ Step 5 · the clock is a guess4 s a breath: 51:00 (9 minutes short) 5 s a breath: 60:00 6 s a breath: 69:00 (9 minutes over)

Run your own class. Choose "My own sequence". The box starts as a copy of rung 5. Replace it with your own, one item per line: a code or name, then R or L, the breaths, and xN for rounds. Codes are in the library at the bottom of the bench. Write down your eight tiles.

★ Step 6 · your classeasy 15 catcow 10 dog sunA x3 w1 R 5 w2 R 5 ... rest CLASS TIME ____ UNITS ____ FLOOR TRIPS ____ UNEVEN ____ UNCOUNTERED ____ PEAK ____% COOL-DOWN ____

Read it in this order. Uncountered backbends and uneven sides first (they are about the body), floor trips second (the room), class time last (the clock). A class that runs two minutes long is an inconvenience; a class with an uncountered Camel is a habit to break.

Part 2 · covers the five-rung ladder

What the ladder teaches

4 min live

Lay the five rungs side by side and a pattern shows. Every fix improved one check; two fixes made the class longer; only one step (the cut) shortened it. That is how drafting a flow usually feels: correct first, then fit.

LiveCorrect first, then fit2 min▶

A common instinct is to fix the time first, because it is the number the studio cares about. The ladder shows why that backfires: trimming rung 1 to an hour would have kept its uneven Warrior II and its three uncountered backbends, and rung 3's counter-poses would then have pushed it over again. Fix what the body needs, fix the path, then cut to fit, choosing cuts that do not undo the first two.

Self-studyWhy rung 4 got longer cool-down for free2 min▶

In rung 3 the last effort-3 item before rest is a Bridge near the end, so the cool-down (the unbroken run of effort 2 or less) is 9:15. Rung 4 moves the Bridges ahead of the hip openers and twists, so the run of gentle work at the end grows to 14:20 with no new poses. Order shapes the wave as much as pose choice does; session 5 uses that.

Real world

The class that always runs five minutes over. A familiar pattern with new teachers: the sequence is lovely and it never fits. Usually every fix they have made over the weeks added a few breaths and nothing was ever taken out. Writing the cut list in advance (session 6) is what breaks the habit.

Part 3 · covers the breath-clock break

One second, nine minutes

3 min live

The breath clock is the bench's biggest assumption. Session 2 showed why: adults breathe 12 to 18 times a minute at rest, slow breathing in a seated lab runs at 6, and nobody has measured a moving class. So the bench lets you try 4, 5 and 6 seconds a breath and watch what happens to the same sequence.

LiveWhy the swing is so large2 min▶

Rung 5 has 484 breath-counted hold breaths and 56 transition units, 540 breaths in all. Each extra second on every breath adds 540 seconds: nine minutes. The 10-minute talk budget and the 5-minute final rest do not move, which is why the swing is the same nine minutes in both directions. A longer, slower class has an even bigger swing.

Self-studyPlanning for a clock you cannot know2 min▶

You cannot know in advance how fast a given room will breathe, and it will change across the hour. Three habits help. Plan at the middle value and write a cut list for running slow (session 6). Time yourself teaching the sequence once out loud, because your own pace while talking is a better guide than any number. And put your flexible items, the extra round and the second pass, in the middle of the class, where they can be dropped without touching the cool-down or the rest.

Real world

The Monday room and the Friday room. Teachers often say the same sequence "takes longer" on some nights. A tired room after work moves and breathes more slowly; a room of regulars who know the flow moves faster. Neither is wrong. A plan that only works on one clock is the fragile part.

Homework

Before session 5

20 min
  1. Run your full class on the bench. Fix anything uneven or uncountered first, then floor trips, then time. Keep a note of each change and what it did.
  2. Run it at 4 and 6 seconds a breath. Write down both totals. That is your swing.
  3. Teach the first 15 minutes out loud with a timer. Compare your real time with the bench at 5 seconds. Which way were you off?
  4. Find your peak. Note which item the bench calls the peak and at what percent. Session 5 asks whether that is where you meant it to be.
